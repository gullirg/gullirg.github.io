I am a PhD student at the [Institute of Cognitive Neuroscience](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/icn/) at UCL, supervised by Prof. Neil Burgess in the [Space & Memory Group](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/icn/research/research-groups/space-memory). My thesis focuses on **spatial and conceptual representations** in the brain.

My academic path has been deliberately interdisciplinary: I hold a BA in Philosophy from Università Vita-Salute San Raffaele in Milan, a BSc in Theoretical Physics (First-Class Honours) from King's College London, and an MSc in Machine Learning (Merit) from UCL, where my thesis explored manifold alignment of graph embeddings in hyperbolic space under Prof. Bradley Love. What ties it all together is a deep fascination with understanding how human thought and consciousness can arise from the brain's matter.

## Research & Experience

Before starting my PhD, I was a Research Assistant in the Space & Memory Group (2022–2023), investigating the role of neural firing rate adaptation effects on hexadirectional modulation. During my time at KCL I was an Undergraduate Research Fellow — awarded the **King's Experience Research Award** — investigating robustness of Turing patterns in reaction-diffusion systems on complex networks. I also worked as a Material Analyst at **Fermilab**, studying surface roughness in nitrogen-doped SRF cavities. At the **Hebrew University of Jerusalem** I interned with the Burak Lab, developing a ring attractor network model of place cells.

## Entrepreneurship

While studying Physics I co-founded **Mappin Technologies**, a startup building indoor navigation solutions for large indoor venues. We were part of the **Kings20** and **Geovation** accelerator cohorts.

## Interests

At the intersection of neuroscience, physics, and machine learning, I am particularly interested in computational models of spatial cognition — how grid cells, place cells, and related circuits give rise to cognitive maps and memory.
